In the beginning, there was the Software Sustainability Institute (SSI),
which encouraged and helped found the creation of the UK Research Software
Engineers Association in 2013-2014, and later a grant for the RSE Network provided,
amongst other things, the funding for an annual conference which began in 2016.
International attendees at the conference and the later ones started their own
RSE organizations, and the UK RSE Association became a Society.

<!--break-->

In 2017-2018, the SSI began work to make RSE sustainable by ensuring that the RSE
community could exist entirely independently of it. Up to 2020, new national RSE
organisations were supported informally with the SSI acting as the repository of
knowledge on how to set up and run a national organisation. In 2020, members of
several national RSE associations and initiatives organized the 2nd International
RSE Leaders Meeting. A breakout group at this meeting, led by
Ian A. Cosden (then-Chair, US-RSE) and Paul Richmond (then-President, Society for RSE),
began to discuss the need for a venue for RSE organizations to talk to each other 
1) to avoid conflicts with dates,
2) to coordinate about joint activities,
3) support each other and fairly resolve conflicts, and
4) to encourage other RSE communities to form.

Together, the group drafted the first version of a charter for an International Council of RSE Associations,
which was subsequently discussed and refined by meeting participants.
See <https://researchsoftware.org/2021/01/27/introducing-the-international-council-of-RSE-associations.html>.

The council started in 2021 with two members from each organization
(see <https://github.com/rse-council/researchsoftware.org/commit/7116d29e5bf6d48e87e3cfc438b96ba0b626e67b> for the original members),
who have changed often, and a [charter](https://github.com/rse-council/researchsoftware.org/blob/main/council/charter.md),
which has been unchanged. As an example of cooperative, multinational RSE advocacy the council decided
to declare the second Thursday in October each year as International RSE Day.
The first International RSE Day was celebrated on 14 October 2021.

Initially, the council didn't have any schedule of regular meetings or leader/chair. It used a rotating
model, where in each meeting, a leader would be chosen for the next meeting who would be responsible
for picking a date (often via a poll of other members), setting up a virtual meeting channel (e.g., zoom),
determining an agenda, and then hosting the meeting.

This process worked for a while, but started to break down especially as founding members rotated off
and new members rotated in, as it was dependent on the selected leader from each meeting ensuring that
a new leader was selected for the next meeting, and that leader doing what they said they would do.

In 2024, The Research Software Alliance (ReSA) volunteered to act as the secretariat for the council,
via an email from Michelle Barker, ReSA director, to Simon Hetrick, who also wanted to ensure that the
council was working. Anna-Lena Lamprecht, who was a de-RSE delegate to the council, brought this to the
council, and reported back to ReSA: "Not surprisingly, there was broad consensus for accepting the
fantastic offer by ReSA!"

When ReSA offered to help the Council by providing the secretariat, members at the time wanted the
meetings to continue to be informal, mostly ad-hoc chats, with the occasional talk from a member,
so things have continued in this vein, as of late 2026.

As Secretariat, ReSA provides support to help the meetings happen, and the communications around that.
In doing that, ReSA has at times filled some gaps to keep things moving, like accepting member
suggestions on what to discuss at the next meeting (because there is no mechanism for a Council member/s to do this).

The Council remains responsible for its own agenda, decision making, coordination, etc.
All leadership regarding the aims and scope of the
Council, and content and format of the meetings, remains the responsibility of the Council.
